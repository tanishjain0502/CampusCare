ABSTRACT
The CampusCare: Centralized Campus Grievance Redressal and Incident Management System is an automated enterprise web solution engineered to address the inefficiencies, lack of transparency, administrative bottlenecks, and traceability risks associated with conventional manual campus complaint handling. In large educational institutions, students and faculty traditionally communicate infrastructure and logistical breakdowns—such as classroom projector defects, laboratory instrument failures, Wi-Fi connectivity dropouts, electrical hazards, sanitation issues, or hostel maintenance requests—via paper registers, suggestion boxes, or unorganized verbal intimations. These legacy practices frequently result in lost tickets, unaccountable delays, zero status visibility, and uncoordinated facility maintenance.
To comprehensively eliminate these operational vulnerabilities, CampusCare establishes a unified, cloud-ready digital platform. The system architecture is built on a multi-layered framework featuring a responsive ReactJS frontend styled with Tailwind CSS and Bootstrap, an asynchronous, high-throughput Node.js / Express REST API backend, and a robust document-oriented database using MongoDB with Mongoose ODM. The authentication mechanism integrates industry-standard JSON Web Tokens (JWT) coupled with Bcrypt password salting to enforce strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) across Students and Administrators.
The system workflow encompasses a self-service registration and authentication pipeline, a dynamic incident reporting module that generates unique alphanumeric tracking codes (e.g., CC-2026-XXXX), categorical routing across nine campus operational domains, and an automated audit lifecycle tracker. Administrators and facility supervisors are provided with an interactive management dashboard displaying real-time operational statistics, priority breakdown matrices, and quick-filter queues.
Through an immutable status history audit log (SUBMITTED → IN_PROGRESS → RESOLVED), CampusCare ensures complete administrative accountability, providing time-stamped resolution remarks for every action. Ultimately, this project demonstrates a modern, scalable, and secure institutional governance solution that saves valuable institutional hours, streamlines campus maintenance, and maximizes student satisfaction.

INTRODUCTION
1.1   Overview
The CampusCare System is a modern, automated web-based solution engineered to replace traditional, manual ways of handling campus grievances and maintenance incidents. In most colleges, universities, and academic institutions, complaint registration is still handled through physical registers placed in administrative offices, suggestion boxes outside hostel wardens' cabins, or uncoordinated verbal communications. These obsolete methods are time-consuming, unorganized, highly prone to human oversight, and completely lack traceability.
In traditional environments, students frequently lodge complaints regarding broken laboratory equipment, non-functional classroom projectors, internet or Wi-Fi connectivity blackouts, hostel water supply failures, or electrical faults, only for those requests to sit unattended for days without any verifiable record of who received them or what corrective actions were scheduled. To permanently resolve this problem, we have developed CampusCare—a centralized campus incident management and grievance redressal platform.
With the rapid evolution of modern web architectures, cloud databases, and single-page applications, digital incident dispatching has become the gold standard in corporate and smart-campus environments. Applying full-stack web technologies to campus grievance management is a vital, natural, and highly practical step toward institutional transparency, accountability, and operational excellence.
1.2   Objectives
The primary goal of this project is to build an intelligent, centralized campus issue resolution system that completely eliminates the inefficiencies of physical registers and verbal complaint channels. The specific objectives are as follows:
  • To Eliminate Paper-Based Registers: To replace cumbersome physical complaint registers and manual suggestion boxes with an intuitive, cloud-accessible digital grievance submission portal that operates 24/7.
  • To Prevent Lost or Ignored Complaints: To guarantee that every single student grievance is automatically assigned a unique tracking ticket number and recorded in a centralized database, ensuring zero lost tickets.

1.3   Problem Statement
In many higher educational institutions, facility maintenance and student grievance handling continue to rely on manual, fragmented workflows. These obsolete mechanisms produce multiple systemic failures:
Current limitations in conventional campus management include:
  1. Lack of Traceability: Complaints written in physical registers can be misplaced, damaged, or arbitrarily ignored with no record of who logged or received them.
  2. Zero Status Transparency: Students have no mechanism to check whether their grievance has been acknowledged, assigned to a technician, or resolved, forcing repeated in-person visits to administrative offices.
  3. Absence of Categorical Prioritization: Critical safety or electrical hazards get buried beneath routine maintenance requests due to the lack of algorithmic priority filtering.
  4. No Institutional Accountability: Departmental heads and college administrators lack unified analytics to evaluate average resolution times, recurring infrastructural failures, or staff performance.
  5. Manual Communication Delays: Information transfer between students, administrative officers, and maintenance technicians involves slow, error-prone manual handoffs.

1.4   Problem Solution
CampusCare replaces slow, opaque manual registers with an instant, full-stack digital incident management platform. Students can securely submit grievances from any smartphone or browser in under 30 seconds. The backend engine automatically generates a verifiable tracking code, routes the request to the appropriate department, logs timestamped status history, and provides administrators with real-time resolution controls.
Step 1: Student Onboarding and Authentication (One-Time Setup):
Students register using their academic credentials, institutional email, and roll number. The system verifies input patterns, hashes the password using Bcrypt, and issues a stateless JSON Web Token (JWT) for secure, authenticated session handling across all subsequent requests.
Step 2: Instant Grievance Lodging (Categorical Classification):
When an issue occurs, the student accesses the Submit Complaint portal. The student inputs a concise title, selects the operational category (e.g., Internet / Wi-Fi, Electrical / Maintenance, Hostel), picks a priority level (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH), specifies the campus location (e.g., Lab 3, Rack 2), and provides a descriptive explanation.
Step 3: Automated Ticket Allocation & Audit Logging:
The backend receives the sanitized payload, validates data types, and generates a unique, human-readable ticket number (e.g., CC-2026-0014). It writes the complaint into the MongoDB database with default status SUBMITTED and pushes an immutable initial audit entry into the statusHistory array.
Step 4: Administrative Review, Dispatch, and Status Transition:
Administrative officers log into the dedicated Admin Dashboard. High-priority incidents are highlighted in red badges. Administrators can inspect ticket details, assign maintenance personnel, update the status to IN_PROGRESS, and record formal resolution notes upon completing the maintenance work.
Step 5: Transparent Resolution and Closed-Loop Verification:
Once maintenance is complete, the admin marks the status as RESOLVED, appending the completion remarks and resolution timestamp. The student's portal updates in real time, displaying the green resolution checkmark and full audit timeline.

2.1   System Architecture
The architecture of the CampusCare System is designed as a multi-layered framework that ensures a smooth flow of data from the client browser interface to permanent database storage. It follows a modular, decoupled approach, strictly separating the user interface, business logic processing, authentication security, and persistent document management.
1. Frontend Layer (User Interface): ReactJS + Tailwind CSS
ReactJS is used to build the responsive client interface. It establishes a Single Page Application (SPA) architecture where dynamic components re-render instantly without triggering full page reloads, providing a desktop-grade user experience.
Tailwind CSS and Bootstrap provide modern, utility-first styling, ensuring responsive grid layouts, crisp typography, and adaptive components across mobile, tablet, and desktop viewports.
Main Pages and Functional Modules:
  • Login / Register Page: Dual-mode public authentication gateway supporting self-registration for students and secure credential validation for administrators.
  • Student Dashboard: Central overview card displaying total complaints submitted, active investigations, and successfully resolved tickets.
  • Submit Complaint Page: Dynamic interactive form with input validation, category dropdowns, priority switches, and description counters.
  • My Complaints Page: Filterable data list with real-time status badges (Blue for Submitted, Amber for In Progress, Green for Resolved).
  • Complaint Details View: Expandable audit timeline illustrating the exact historical lifecycle, supervisor notes, and timestamps.
  • Admin Dashboard & Management Portal: Executive control cockpit providing aggregate KPI counters, priority queues, and modal dialogs to update complaint statuses.

2. Backend Layer: Node.js & Express REST API Server
Express.js serves as the central server-side web application framework running on the Node.js runtime. It orchestrates HTTP request routing, input sanitization, controller logic execution, security enforcement, and database communications.
Main Architectural Layers:
  1. Controller Layer: Receives REST API requests (GET, POST, PUT, DELETE) from the ReactJS frontend, validates incoming JSON payloads, invokes domain business logic, and returns formatted JSON responses with standard HTTP status codes.
  2. Middleware Layer: Intercepts all protected routes to verify JWT bearer tokens, extracts the authenticated user context, checks role permissions (STUDENT vs ADMIN), and sanitizes inputs against malicious payloads.
  3. Service & Business Logic Layer: Contains algorithmic operations including unique ticket number generation (CC-YYYY-XXXX), status transition enforcement rules, and status history array appending.
  4. Repository & ODM Layer: Utilizes Mongoose ODM to enforce strict document schemas, handle compound indexing, and communicate with the underlying MongoDB database.
3. Data Storage Layer (MongoDB NoSQL Database)
  • Employs MongoDB as a high-performance document store, efficiently handling nested structures like complaint status history and user authentication details.
  • Establishes resilient connection pooling with auto-reconnect logic via Mongoose.
  • Enforces database-level indexes on createdBy, status, category, and ticketNumber to ensure lightning-fast retrieval times under institutional load.
4. Output & Analytics Layer (Reporting Engine)
  • Computes real-time aggregate statistics for the administrator dashboard (Total, In Progress, Resolved, High Priority).
  • Enables real-time data inspection for audit compliance, departmental review, and infrastructure maintenance planning.

3.2   Backend Architecture
The Backend represents the core operational and business logic engine of the CampusCare System. It is built using Node.js and Express.js—a high-performance, asynchronous JavaScript runtime and web framework optimized for building scalable RESTful APIs.
Express.js serves as the central API gateway connecting the ReactJS client application with the MongoDB database cluster, enforcing authentication, request validation, business rules, and error logging.
Layers of Express / Node.js Backend:
1. Controller Layer (authController.js & complaintController.js)
  • Receives all incoming HTTP requests transmitted from the React frontend.
  • Validates request parameters and payload structure before invoking database services.
  • Returns standardized JSON responses accompanied by semantic HTTP status codes (200 OK, 201 Created, 400 Bad Request, 401 Unauthorized, 403 Forbidden, 404 Not Found, 500 Server Error).
2. Middleware Layer (authMiddleware.js & roleMiddleware.js)
  • Extracts the Authorization: Bearer <token> HTTP header.
  • Verifies cryptographic token signatures against the server's private secret key.
  • Attaches the decoded user profile (ID, Role) to the req.user context.
  • Enforces role guard rails: Blocks non-admin users from accessing administrative incident queues or modification endpoints.
3. Model & ODM Layer (Mongoose)
  • Defines schema definitions for User and Complaint documents.
  • Applies automatic createdAt and updatedAt timestamps.
  • Transforms JSON output to strip sensitive internal properties like passwordHash and __v.

3.3   Database Management (MongoDB)
For the CampusCare System, the database layer (MongoDB) acts as the secure, persistent memory of the platform. It reliably stores user credentials, incident tickets, status audit entries, and categorical configurations across academic sessions.
1. Structural Organization (The Collections)
The database is organized into structured collections with strict schema validation to ensure clean, consistent records:
  • Users Collection: Master repository of system accounts. Stores unique user IDs, full names, lowercase unique email addresses, Bcrypt password hashes, and assigned system roles (STUDENT or ADMIN).
  • Complaints Collection: Core repository of all campus grievances. Stores unique ticket numbers (e.g., CC-2026-0014), foreign reference to author (createdBy), title, operational category, priority, current status, location, resolution remarks, and completion timestamps.
  • Status History Subdocuments: Embedded array inside each complaint document capturing every state transition: previous status, new status, supervisor user reference, change timestamp, and operational remarks.
2. Database Indexing & Query Optimization
To maintain sub-5 millisecond query execution times even as institutional complaint records scale into tens of thousands of documents, CampusCare implements compound indexing:
  • { ticketNumber: 1 } (Unique): Enables instant ticket lookups.
  • { createdBy: 1, createdAt: -1 }: Optimizes student personal grievance history queries.
  • { status: 1, createdAt: -1 }: Accelerates administrative dashboard queue filtering.
3. Connection Architecture & Singleton Pattern
The server establishes a resilient singleton connection pool to MongoDB via Mongoose. It automatically manages connection retry cycles, handles network interruptions gracefully, and prevents connection leaks.

3.6   Security and Scalability
Security Features
The CampusCare architecture implements multiple defensive layers to ensure total student privacy, data integrity, and protection against unauthorized administrative operations:
  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Explicit separation of privileges. Students can strictly submit complaints and view their own tickets. Administrative endpoints (updating ticket statuses, adding resolution notes, viewing global analytics) strictly require validated ADMIN tokens.
  • Credential Protection & Salting: Passwords are never stored in plain text. Each password is cryptographically salted and hashed using Bcrypt with 10 salt rounds before persistence. Mongoose schemas designate select: false for the password hash field to prevent accidental leakage in user queries.
  • Input Sanitization & Injection Immunity: Parameterized NoSQL queries via Mongoose prevent NoSQL injection attacks. Strict schema enumerations guarantee that malicious users cannot inject arbitrary status values or undefined complaint categories.
  • Stateless Authorization: JWT tokens have defined expiration windows, eliminating session hijacking risks and eliminating the need for server-side session memory stores.
Scalability
The application is architected to scale effortlessly as college enrollment and facility demands expand:
  • Stateless Micro-Services: Because authentication relies on self-contained JWT tokens, the Node.js backend can be horizontally scaled across multiple instances behind a load balancer (e.g., NGINX) without session state synchronization issues.
  • Asynchronous Event Loop: Node.js processes I/O operations non-blockingly, allowing a single lightweight server instance to handle hundreds of concurrent student submissions simultaneously.
  • Optimized Database Indexing: Compound MongoDB indexes ensure that query execution time remains constant (O(log N)) even when historical grievance logs exceed 100,000 documents.

3.7   Security Measures
The CampusCare System incorporates rigorous security measures to safeguard student confidential data, prevent malicious record tampering, and guarantee institutional compliance:
1. Tamper-Proof Audit Trail (statusHistory)
  • Whenever a complaint transitions between states (SUBMITTED → IN_PROGRESS → RESOLVED), the backend appends an immutable log entry into the statusHistory array.
  • Each log entry permanently records the exact timestamp, the previous state, the new state, the user ID of the administrator who executed the transition, and the accompanying operational note.
  • Once logged, historical entries cannot be edited or erased, ensuring complete dispute-free traceability.
2. Strict Data Isolation Between Students
  • API queries for student complaints strictly filter by { createdBy: req.user.id } extracted directly from the verified JWT payload, not from client request parameters.
  • This completely prevents Insecure Direct Object Reference (IDOR) vulnerabilities—a student can never view or manipulate another student's grievance record.
3. Administrative Seed Isolation
  • There is zero public endpoint that allows users to register with an ADMIN role.
  • Administrator accounts can only be provisioned through secure, server-side initialization scripts (npm run seed:admin) executed directly in the host server environment with administrative credentials.
4. Centralized Exception Handling & Sanitized Responses
  • A global error handling middleware intercepts all runtime exceptions, logging detailed stack traces to secure server logs while returning clean, sanitized error messages to the client.
  • Prevents internal database paths or server architecture details from leaking to end users.

The implementation of the CampusCare System followed a structured Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC), moving systematically from requirements specification and database schema modeling to full-stack API integration, user interface development, and comprehensive multi-tier testing.
4.1   Implementation

  • Implemented student registration with normalized lowercase email verification and Bcrypt cryptographic hashing.
  • Implemented JWT token generation signed with HMAC-SHA256, carrying user IDs and role claims.

3. Real-Time Status Lifecycle Engine

CHAPTER 5. CONCLUSION 2026
Chapter 5
CONCLUSION
The CampusCare: Centralized Campus Grievance Redressal and Incident Management System provides a robust, efficient, and modern solution to the long-standing challenges of campus facility maintenance and student grievance redressal. By leveraging modern full-stack web technologies—including ReactJS, Node.js, Express, and MongoDB—the project successfully transitions educational institutions from slow, paper-based registers to a high-speed, transparent digital workflow.
5.1   Project Summary
This project introduces a centralized incident management platform designed to replace antiquated suggestion boxes and physical complaint registers with an intuitive, cloud-ready web portal. Students can report infrastructural breakdowns, sanitation issues, electrical faults, laboratory deficiencies, and academic facility concerns in seconds. The system automatically tags incidents, generates unique tracking numbers, and securely logs them into a high-performance database.
The architecture follows a modular, multi-layered design—featuring a clean ReactJS frontend, an asynchronous Node.js/Express API layer, and a scalable MongoDB document store—to deliver a seamless experience for both students and college administrators. Administrative authorities benefit from real-time dashboard analytics, priority queue management, and an immutable status history audit trail. Ultimately, CampusCare delivers a transparent, scalable, and touchless governance solution that enhances accountability while saving valuable educational and administrative time.
5.2   Achievements
The project successfully attained multiple functional and technical milestones during design, development, and testing:
  • Centralized Institutional Gateway: Successfully consolidated complaints from nine distinct campus operational domains into a single unified management cockpit.
  • High-Speed Processing: Sub-second complaint submission and real-time dashboard metrics computation.
  • 100% Traceability & Accountability: Complete elimination of misplaced grievances through unique ticket generation and immutable status history logs.

REFERENCES
The development of the CampusCare: Centralized Campus Grievance Redressal and Incident Management System is primarily built upon modern web engineering principles, robust distributed database paradigms, and asynchronous API architectures. To achieve responsive and accessible client interfaces, the project utilizes the open-source ReactJS framework developed by Meta, paired with the utility-based styling framework Tailwind CSS.
The technical backend framework is anchored in Node.js and Express.js, which deliver high-throughput, non-blocking asynchronous event handling to bridge client HTTP requests with enterprise business rules. Furthermore, the system relies on MongoDB with Mongoose ODM to maintain a secure, highly indexable document database, ensuring that every student incident, priority queue, and status transition log is accurately stored, audited, and protected. Stateless authorization is implemented according to RFC 7519 JSON Web Token (JWT) architecture with Bcrypt cryptographic password hashing.
